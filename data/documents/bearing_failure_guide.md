# Bearing Failure Analysis Guide

## Common Root Causes
1. Lubrication failure (contamination, wrong grade, insufficient quantity)
2. Misalignment between motor and driven equipment
3. Unbalance in rotating assembly
4. Overloading or cavitation (pumps)
5. Electrical fluting on motor bearings (VFD-driven equipment)

## Diagnostic Workflow
Step 1: Review trend data for vibration, temperature, and operating hours.
Step 2: Compare readings to equipment-specific SOP thresholds.
Step 3: Perform onsite inspection — listen for noise, check for heat, look for leaks.
Step 4: Decide: monitor, inspect, or replace.

## Vibration Frequency Hints
- 1× running speed: unbalance, misalignment
- 2× running speed: misalignment, looseness
- High-frequency peaks: bearing defect frequencies (BPFI, BPFO)
- Blade pass frequency: impeller or fan issues

## When to Replace vs. Monitor
Monitor if vibration is stable, below warning threshold, and temperature is normal.
Replace if:
- Vibration is rising >10% per week
- Temperature exceeds critical threshold
- Visible spalling, noise, or play in bearing

## Documentation Requirements
All maintenance decisions must be logged with:
- Date and time
- Sensor readings at time of decision
- Action taken
- Parts used
- Technician name

This supports audit trails and improves future predictive models.

## Spare Parts Lead Time
Standard ball bearings: 3–5 business days.
Specialty ceramic bearings: 2–3 weeks. Plan high-risk assets accordingly.
